From 1.13.0 you can set up a new device from one you already use. You check that both
screens show the same four emoji, tap Approve, and the new device has your keys, your
servers and your encrypted DM history.

## What went wrong before

Your DM keys come from your 24 words. Until now there were two ways to get them onto a
second device. You typed the words in, or you set a message password on the first device
and entered it on the second.

If you did neither, the second device made up its own 24 words. It was the same account
with different keys, and for a while nothing said so. The people you talk to had already
pinned your old key, so their app saw it change and stopped encrypting. Their banner said
"This conversation isn't encrypted. Whoever runs this server can read it. Somebody here
changed their key." Your banner still said encrypted, and their messages showed up red
with a warning. That's what I saw in one of my own DMs. And on the new device, anything
sent to the old one said "This message could not be opened."

## How you link one

On the new device, pick **Link with another device**. It's in Settings, Account, and on
the welcome screen when you aren't signed in. On the phone it's **Link from another
device**. It shows a QR code and an 8-character code like `7KQM-X4TD`.

Then, on a device you already use:

- **On the phone**, go to Preferences, Security, **Link a new device**, and scan the QR.
  Or tap "Type a code instead".
- **On the desktop**, go to Settings, Account & security, **Link a new device**, and type
  the code. Most desktops don't have a camera to point at a phone. Lower case is fine,
  and so is `O` for `0`.

Both screens now show the same four emoji, with their names under them. The device
you're approving from also shows what the new one says it is, and roughly where each of
you is: "MacBook Air, Gryt desktop on macOS. Near Oslo, Norway. You're near Bergen,
Norway." If the emoji match, tap Approve. On the phone that asks for Face ID, Touch ID or
your passcode first.

You get 60 seconds to approve. After that it runs out and the new device shows a fresh
code. An unused code also gets swapped for a new one every five minutes.

A server takes five devices per person at most. If the new one would be a sixth, the
approval screen says which server and asks you to remove one first.

After Approve there's nothing else to do. For an account, the new device signs in on its
own, with no password and no browser. It joins your servers, and your other device adds
it to every DM.

## What gets sent, and who can read it

Both devices make a one-off key for this link and work out a shared secret from the two.
Everything after that is sealed with it, end to end. When you tap Approve, your device
sends one sealed envelope with:

- the seed your 24 words come from
- which account you are, if you have one, so the new device can check it signed in as you
- your servers, and the keys you've pinned for the people on them
- a key for your message history

Your sign-in isn't in it. The new device gets a session of its own, so signing out on one
leaves the other signed in. Your MLS state isn't in it either. The new device makes its
own and gets added to each DM as a separate device.

In the middle is the relay, at id.gryt.chat. It passes along bytes it can't open. It
keeps each link in memory, not on disk, and its logs have counts and durations but no
addresses, codes or locations.

Your history goes across in chunks, newest day first, sealed with the key from the
envelope. The relay holds them on disk until the new device fetches them, and deletes
them after an hour either way. The list of chunks, with a hash for each, goes over the
sealed channel. So the relay can't drop, swap or replay a chunk without the new device
seeing it.

The relay does see both devices' IP addresses, when you linked, and how much history went
across, which says roughly how many messages you have. For an account, Keycloak sees a
sign-in at the same moment, so whoever runs both could tie the two together by time.
That's me.

## What the emoji are for

To read the envelope, the relay would have to swap in keys of its own on both sides. The
emoji come from both devices' keys, so a swapped key gives the two screens different
emoji. It can't hunt for a swap that happens to match either. The new device commits to
its key before it sees the other one, so the relay gets a single guess per link, a 1 in
16.7 million chance. If the emoji differ, tap "They don't match" and nothing gets sent.

The emoji can't help if somebody talks you into approving a device of theirs, since their
screen shows the same emoji yours does. For that, the QR only works in Gryt's own scanner,
so your phone's camera app can't open an approval screen. The approval screen says the
device gets your messages, your keys and your account, and shows where it is. And Gryt
never asks you to scan a code from somebody else's device or a website.

If a device gets linked anyway, your other devices tell you. They show "New device linked"
with its name, and a Remove button right there.

## Guests too

A guest's identity on each server is worked out from the seed. So the new device gets the
seed and your server list, and each server sees the same guest it always did. Guests skip
the sign-in step, and the relay can't tell whether you're one.

## What it doesn't do yet

- **Scanning with a webcam on the desktop.** You type the code there for now.
- **Carrying on after a restart.** If either app closes while history is still copying,
  the copy stops. Your keys and sign-in are done by then. Linking again from the same
  device sends the rest, and nothing shows up twice.
- **Backups.** Linking copies your history from one device to another. There's no backup
  of it anywhere yet.

No dates on any of those.
