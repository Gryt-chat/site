Gryt has had an owl since 2023. It was the logo, it sat in the corner of the
login page, and for a while it was a tiny astronaut in some promo art. It was
never anybody in particular.

It is now. Everyone on a Gryt server who has not uploaded a picture is drawn as
an owl, worked out from their nickname, and the whole thing is a package called
[`@gryt/owl`](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@gryt/owl): give it a name, get an
SVG. No renderer, no DOM, no dependencies.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-sheet.webp" alt="Seventy-two Gryt owls in a grid, each on its own coloured circle — different palettes, expressions, glasses, hats, scarves and jackets" width="2784" height="1440" />

## Why it is not DiceBear

The obvious way to build this is DiceBear. It is good, it is free, and it has a
dozen styles that would have taken an afternoon to wire up.

I tried it and took it out again, because DiceBear's model is a style definition
made of interchangeable sprite layers, and that is not what I wanted. What I
wanted was one drawn character with variations. The difference shows up in a
member list: layered sprites read as a sticker sheet, and a single character with
different colours and hats reads as one product.

So the bird never varies. The body, the wings, the face plate and the beak are
fixed numbers on a 1024 artboard, and they are the same numbers for everyone.
What the seed picks is the palette, whether there are ear tufts, and what the
bird is wearing.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-layers.webp" alt="The same teal owl five times, each adding one thing: bare, then a wink, then round glasses, then a knitted hat, then a scarf" width="2952" height="648" />

Fixed geometry is also what makes the accessories work at all. A pair of glasses
is pinned to absolute coordinates, and it lands on the eyes because that is where
the eyes are — on every owl, forever.

## The same seed draws the same owl

That has to hold on every client and across every upgrade. A person is
recognised by their avatar, so an owl that shifts when the library is bumped has
failed at the one job it has.

Two consequences that look like fussiness and are not. Every random draw is keyed
on a name rather than a position, so adding a new hat does not reshuffle the
hats that were already there. And nothing depends on the platform: no
`Math.random`, no `Date`, no `Intl`, no DOM. The tsconfig drops the DOM library
on purpose, so reaching for `document` is a compile error instead of a crash on
somebody's phone.

Three seeds are pinned in the tests against their exact output. When one of those
fails, the question is not "what is the new value" but "is every existing user
about to look different."

## Thirty palettes

Ten hues in three schemes — night, day and dusk. The hues are not picked by eye;
they come from `TILE_HUES`, the list a voice tile's tint snaps to. So the colour
of your avatar and the colour of your tile in a call are the same colour rather
than two shades that nearly match.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-palettes.webp" alt="One seed drawn in all thirty palettes: ten hues across three rows labelled night, day and dusk" width="2532" height="1028" />

An accessory names a colour *role* rather than carrying a hex value, which is why
a teal owl and a pink owl can wear the same hat in their own colours.

## Fifty drawings, and none of them are path data

There are five slots — expression, glasses, head, neck and clothes — rolled
independently, so a hat and glasses and a scarf can all turn up at once. A slot
can also come up empty; empty is the most common outcome in every one of them.
Right now there are fifty drawings across the five.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-wardrobe.webp" alt="Every Gryt owl accessory grouped by slot on the same teal bird: 19 expressions, 8 pairs of glasses, 18 hats, 3 neck pieces and 2 items of clothing" width="2992" height="2560" />

Adding one does not involve writing SVG path data or editing a manifest. You
export the bare bird, draw on top of it, and save the file with a name that says
what it is. `Winter_Hat.svg` is a hat because "hat" is in the filename.
`Heart_Glasses.rare.svg` is eyewear that turns up less often than the rest. The
filename is the configuration.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-drawing.webp" alt="The 'Drawing a cosmetic' walkthrough on ui.gryt.chat, showing the bare bird, the same bird with a puffer jacket drawn over it, and the finished jacket recoloured onto a gold palette" width="2880" height="1900" />

Two things travel with the drawing without being written down anywhere. Which of
the bird's own layers you *hid* is which parts the accessory replaces, so a wink
is one hidden eye and a coat is two hidden arms. And where you put the drawing
relative to the bird's group says whether it is worn in front or behind, which is
how a headset gets its band behind the head.

Colours are not configuration either. One table maps the colours accessories are
drawn in to palette roles, so a drawing that reuses colours already in use needs
nothing at all. A colour nothing knows about stops the build and prints the line
to add.

If you want to check a drawing before opening a pull request, there is a
command that needs nothing but Node:

```sh
npx @gryt/owl check my-hat.svg
```

It tells you whether the filename gives it a slot, whether it is on the 1024
frame, whether the transforms are flattened, how much of the bird it found, and
what is left once the bird is subtracted back out. It exits non-zero when the
drawing would not build, so it can hang off a commit hook.

## Draw one

This is the bird you start from.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-figma-base.webp" alt="The bare Gryt owl with no accessories and no background, the base drawing an accessory is made on top of" width="1000" height="1000" />

Most of the fifty were drawn in Figma against **[Build A Gryt](https://www.figma.com/community/file/1674379263156144233/build-a-gryt)**,
a community file that carries that bird as a component, with a layer per part
and a walkthrough. Open it, duplicate it, draw a hat on the bird, export, and
the filename does the rest.

If you are not in Figma, one command writes the same bird out as an SVG with the
same layers — `Left Eye`, `Right Arm`, `Nose` — and you can draw on that
instead.

The full walkthrough, with the bird to download and a worked example, is at
[ui.gryt.chat/avatars/drawing](https://ui.gryt.chat/avatars/drawing). The
avatars page next to it draws every owl on it live, and counts how often each
accessory turns up out of three thousand seeds, so the numbers on the page cannot
drift from the weights in the code.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-avatars-page.webp" alt="The Avatars page on ui.gryt.chat: part of its contact sheet above a Colour section showing the same seed in all thirty palettes" width="2880" height="1900" />

## Where they turn up

The web client, the desktop app and the phone app all draw people from this same
file, so your owl is the same owl in all three. Voice tiles take their tint from
it. `@gryt/ui`'s `Avatar` generates one when it has a seed and no image, and
`@gryt/ui-native`'s does the same through `react-native-svg`.

<Image src="/blog/gryt-owls-in-the-client.webp" alt="A Gryt channel in the desktop client, dark theme, with two members drawn as owls in the message list and the member rail" width="2880" height="1800" />

Server icons are still DiceBear Planets. A server is not a person and should not
be drawn as one.

A bare owl is about 2.1 kB of markup, and about 4.1 kB with accessories on.

## You can pick

Everything above describes the owl your name happens to produce. As of this
week you can also choose it — colours, expression, glasses, hat, the lot — from
inside the client. That has [its own post](/blog/designing-your-own-owl).

The package is MIT, like the rest of the UI packages and unlike the AGPL-3.0
apps that use them. `bun add @gryt/owl` and it will draw owls for something that
is not Gryt.
